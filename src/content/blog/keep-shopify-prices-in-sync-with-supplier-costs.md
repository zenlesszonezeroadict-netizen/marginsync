# Keeping Shopify Prices in Sync With Supplier Costs

If you buy stock from real suppliers, your costs are a moving target. A supplier nudges a wholesale price up 6%, you don't notice for two months, and a product you thought earned 40% margin is quietly earning 31%. Keeping Shopify prices in sync with supplier costs is one of the least glamorous parts of running a store — and one of the most expensive to ignore.

This is a guide for merchants with genuine supplier relationships (not dropshippers): why margins erode, how to stay ahead of it, and how to reprice without breaking things.

## Why margins erode quietly

Margin erosion is dangerous precisely because nothing alerts you to it. Your storefront keeps showing the same retail price, orders keep coming in, and the dashboard looks healthy. Meanwhile the gap between what you pay and what you charge has narrowed.

It usually creeps in through:

- **Staggered supplier increases** — a few SKUs at a time, never a big enough jump to notice.
- **New cost lists that sit unread** in your inbox because updating prices is a chore.
- **Partial updates** — you reprice the obvious bestsellers and leave the long tail at old margins.

## A workflow to stay ahead of it

You don't need fancy tooling to keep costs and prices aligned — you need a repeatable habit.

### 1. Treat every supplier cost file as a trigger

When a supplier sends a new price list, that's the cue to reprice — not "someday." Even a monthly cadence beats reacting only when something feels off.

### 2. Work from cost, not gut feel

Decide your target margin per category (say 55% on accessories, 35% on hardware) and calculate retail *from cost*: `retail = cost ÷ (1 − margin)`. This keeps repricing objective instead of guesswork.

### 3. Check the whole catalogue, not just bestsellers

The slow bleed is in the products you don't think about. Whatever method you use, make sure it covers every SKU in the supplier file, not just the ones top of mind.

### 4. Keep a record of what changed

Before applying a batch, know what the old prices were. If a number looks wrong next week, you want to see exactly what changed and when.

## The manual reality

Done by hand, this means matching the supplier's SKUs to your Shopify SKUs in a spreadsheet, applying your margin formula, and importing the result — every time a new cost list lands. It works, but it's tedious enough that most merchants skip it until margins visibly hurt. The whole point of a system is to make repricing cheap enough that you actually do it on schedule.
