# Keeping Shopify Prices in Sync With Supplier Costs

If you buy stock from real suppliers, your costs never sit still. A supplier nudges a wholesale price up 6%, you don't catch it for two months, and a product you thought was making 40% is quietly down to 31%. Keeping Shopify prices in sync with supplier costs is one of the dullest parts of running a store. It's also one of the most expensive to ignore.

This one's for merchants with actual supplier relationships, not dropshippers: why margins slip, how to stay ahead of it, and how to reprice without breaking things.

## Why margins slip without you noticing

The reason margin erosion is dangerous is that nothing flags it for you. Your storefront still shows the same price, orders still come in, the dashboard still looks fine. The only thing that's changed is the gap between what you pay and what you charge, and that's invisible until you go looking.

It tends to creep in three ways:

- Staggered increases. A few SKUs at a time, never a jump big enough to grab your attention.
- Cost lists that sit unread in your inbox, because updating prices is a chore and there's always something more urgent.
- Half-finished updates, where you reprice the obvious bestsellers and leave the long tail sitting at old margins.

## A workflow to stay ahead of it

You don't need fancy software for this. You need a habit you'll actually repeat.

### 1. Let every cost file be the trigger

When a supplier sends a new list, that's your cue to reprice. Not "soon," not "when I get to it." Even a once-a-month rhythm beats only reacting when something already feels wrong.

### 2. Work from cost, not gut feel

Pick a target margin per category (say 55% on accessories, 35% on hardware) and work the retail price back from cost: `retail = cost / (1 - margin)`. It keeps repricing a calculation instead of a guess.

### 3. Cover the whole catalogue

The slow bleed lives in the products you never think about. However you reprice, make sure it touches every SKU on the supplier's list, not just the ones front of mind.

### 4. Keep a record of what moved

Before you apply a batch, know what the old prices were. When a number looks wrong next week, you'll want to see exactly what changed and when.

## What this looks like by hand

Done manually, all of that means matching the supplier's SKUs to yours in a spreadsheet, applying your margin formula, and importing the result, every single time a new cost list shows up. It works. It's also tedious enough that most people put it off until the margins visibly hurt. A good system is really just one that makes repricing cheap enough that you keep doing it on schedule.
