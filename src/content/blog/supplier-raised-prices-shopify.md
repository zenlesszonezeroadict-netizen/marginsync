# What to Do When a Supplier Raises Their Prices

A new cost sheet lands in your inbox and half your products just got more expensive to stock. It happens to every store that buys from real suppliers, and how you react decides whether you protect your margin or quietly absorb the hit for months.

Here's a calm, practical way to handle a supplier price increase on Shopify.

## 1. Work out the real damage first

Before touching any prices, figure out what actually changed. For each affected product, compare the old cost to the new one and check what it does to your margin at your current retail price.

Some increases are small enough to sit on for a while. Others quietly push a product close to break-even. You can't make good decisions until you can see which is which, so start with the numbers, not a gut reaction.

## 2. Decide product by product, not all-or-nothing

You don't have to pass on every increase, and you don't have to eat it either. A few options, depending on the product:

- Pass it through. For price-insensitive or hard-to-find items, raise retail to keep your margin where it was.
- Partially absorb it. Split the increase so your price moves less than your cost did. Easier for customers to swallow.
- Hold the price, drop the product. If an item was already thin and the increase kills the margin, it might be time to stop stocking it.
- Hold the price, find another supplier. Worth doing for your bestsellers before you touch their price at all.

## 3. Round to prices that still look right

If a cost increase pushes a $24.99 product to $26.37, don't list it at $26.37. Round to a price that reads cleanly ($26.99, maybe $27). Odd, precise numbers look like exactly what they are: a reluctant pass-through. Tidy prices feel intentional.

## 4. Make the change cleanly, and keep a record

However you reprice, do it in one deliberate pass rather than dribbling changes in over a week. And keep a record of what the prices were before, so if a number looks wrong later you can see exactly what moved and when.
